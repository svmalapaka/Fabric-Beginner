1. Treating Fabric Like a Traditional Data Warehouse

Many beginners assume Fabric works like a classic SQL warehouse. But Fabric is lake-first — meaning files, tables, notebooks, and SQL all share the same storage.

2. Creating Multiple Copies of the Same Data

OneLake eliminates the need for duplication. Copying data into multiple workspaces or warehouses creates confusion and wastes storage.

3. Ignoring Delta Format

Delta tables are the backbone of Fabric. Beginners often store raw CSVs instead — losing performance, reliability, and ACID transactions.

4. Using Pipelines for Everything

Pipelines are great for scheduled movement, but not for real-time or high-volume ingestion. Use Eventstreams or KQL for streaming workloads.

5. Not Leveraging the Lakehouse

The Lakehouse is the most flexible workspace in Fabric. Beginners often skip it and jump straight to Warehouse, missing out on notebooks, ML, and file-based analytics.

6. Overcomplicating the Architecture

Fabric is designed to simplify analytics. Beginners sometimes recreate old multi-layer architectures (bronze/silver/gold) even when not needed.

7. Forgetting Security Inheritance

OneLake security flows downward. If you secure a folder, everything inside inherits permissions — beginners often overlook this.

8. Mixing Workspace Purposes

Workspaces should be organized by business domain, not by random projects. Mixing unrelated items leads to clutter and permission issues.
